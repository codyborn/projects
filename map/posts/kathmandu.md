A good trek always starts with provisions. What better city to stock up than Kathmandu, the old trading capital of Nepal? Rickshaws run the narrow streets, hawkers sell bamboo flutes and shopkeepers sweep their sidewalks with wooden brooms. If it weren't for the sea of honking motorbikes and occasional white trekker, you'd think you'd gone back in time.

I arrived just in time for the start of Diwali, one of the biggest festivals of the year. Firecrackers peppered the city as processions of performers marched through the streets in vibrant gold and red. In the morning, store fronts were washed and the sidewalk painted with a mixture of paint and colored sands. Street dogs were honored, each given a necklace of marigolds and blessed with a red mark on their forehead.

I met my guide, the person I'd be living with for the next two weeks. In his early thirties, he had been a sherpa for the past eight years. I walked him through my gear and we identified what needed to be added or removed. The whole pack came out to 35 lbs.

I had been accumulating gear for the past couple of months for the trip, but still needed a handful of small things. The hardest thing was actually securing the cash. To get the 50,000 Nepali rupees, I had to stop by seven different ATMs. Many were not working, closed or had tight daily limits.

My day bag full of a wad of cash and a dozen Snickers bars, I was finally ready for the trip. I relaxed and enjoyed a traditional Nepalese thali: a collection of small brass bowls of a variety of foods from pickles to curries surrounding a warm mound of rice.
